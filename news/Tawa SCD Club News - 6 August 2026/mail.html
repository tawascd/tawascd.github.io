In last night's new dancers' section
From this week, I will be including the the new dancers' section in the general club email. 
We started, as usual with a walking warm-up dance, and foot and ankle exercises. Then it was on to learning a new formation - the Reel of Three

Reels of Three
In 3-couple sets: 
To start with, Catherine got each couple, in turn, to walk a figure of 8 along the sides. Partners can touch nearer hands when they come into the middle 
  • The 1s walk a fig 8, by starting in and down.
  • The 2s walk a fig 8, by going out and up
  • The 3s walk a fig 8, by going in and up. 

Then, all three couples walked their figure of 8 path at the same time. This is called a Reel of Three.
 
  • The 1s go in and down, 2s go out and up, 3s go in and up (You will hear this description often when dances are "briefed"). 
  • In effect all three couples are covering the same path, rather like three slot cars running on the same track. 
  • To avoid collisions, the 3s (going in and up) must give way to the 1s (going in and down). 
  • https://youtu.be/OuWzbkrnyvE
  • This formation is also referred to as "mirror reels" or "reflection reels"
  • It can be helpful for couples to hold hands when possible. Some dances actually do specify holding hands as part of the instructions. 

Catherine also reviewed the formation we  learned last week - Rights and Lefts.
  • In each couple, one of you will be going clockwise, the other anti-clockwise. 
  • Across is always right hand;  along the side is always left hand
  • The hands should stay at above waist level - do not let them droop! 
  • When you have done the whole formation, you are back in the same position from where you started. 
  • In the video you will see that two of the people in each square finish with what is called a "courtesy turn". Don't worry about that just yet. Catherine will cover that in due course.

A note about the links to the dances
In the next section, the links I provide mostly come from the SCD Database. Each dance has several tabs: 
  • Overview: provides the name of the deviser, the type of dance and number of couples, and formations
  • Extra info: sometimes has interesting details about the dance
  • Crib/Diagrams: description of the dance. Uses standard abbreviations (a glossary of abbreviations is attached below). You can ignore the diagrams for now
  • Videos: popular dances have lots of videos, not all of them excellent. Look for the ones that are "demonstration" quality. 
  • You can ignore the other tabs for now.


Last night's dances
The first five dances were for all, after which we stopped to have supper. Then the last two dances were more complex, for the existing members. 
  1. Welcome to the Dance (J) - This was our walking warm-up dance. A round-the-room dance. https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/7035/
  2. Across Kinness Burn (R)  - This dance starts with figs of 8 on the sides, followed by reels of 3 on the sides. (I can't find a video for this) 
  3. 12 Coates Crescent (L) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/9106/ The name of this dance is the address of the HQ of the RSCDS (Royal Scottish  Country Dance Society) in Edinburgh. This dance is on the programme of the Waikanae Annual Dance on Saturday. 
  4. The Auld Grey Cat (R) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/295/ Another chance to practise the reels of 3. The second video is the better one to watch. We danced this last week. 
  5. Crabbit Shona (J) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/13259/ We danced this last week. It starts with Rights and Lefts, and finishes with an Allemande.
  6. The Booms of Bon Accord (R) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/584/ Devised by John Drewry. "Bon Accord" is the official historic motto of the city of Aberdeen, in Scotland. This is on Waikanae's Annual Dance programme. 
  7. The Orchards of Co Armagh (S) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/17538/ Also on Waikanae's programme. We danced this last week.
