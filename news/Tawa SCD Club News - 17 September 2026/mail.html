Well, what a horribly wet night it was tonight. Maureen and I thought we'd be lucky to get one set, brave enough to venture out on such a night. But we had a great turnout, considering ... 18 people! Well done everyone!

Maureen was teaching us, and will again next week, while Catherine is enjoying a well-deserved holiday.  She mentioned that Catherine had asked her to teach Pas de Basque and the concept of 'corners' for the new dancers. But in the absence of two of our very new dancers (who have said they will be back!), she did not spend a lot of time on Pas de Basque. We did however do a couple of dances which involved 'corners'. The newer dancers were able to go home after Granville Market. The rest of us did three more dances after that. 


Tonight's dances
  1. The Highland Fair (J) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/2910/ This was our walking warm-up dance. Both this and the next dance started with the 1s casting off behind own lines and cast back. Remember that when "casting back" you turn away from your partner, i.e. facing out.
  2. Corner Meeting (J) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/13790/ Before dancing this, Maureen explained the concept of 'corners'. See details below. This dance was devised by Rod Downey, as a teaching dance, especially for teaching corners. 
  3. Pas de Basque practice (R) - We spent just a short while practising Pas de Basque. See details below. 
  4. Sandy O'er the Lea (S) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/5889/ 
  5. Granville Market (J) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/13621/ This dance is on the programmes of the New Dancers' Celebration (NDC), and of the Glendarroch Annual Dance.
  6. The Reverend John McFarlane (R) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/5555/ This dance commemorates the 150th anniversary of the founding in Wellington of NZ's first Scots Church, in 1840. There is a monument on the Petone foreshore which marks the centenary of this service. This dance is on the programme of the Region Ball, on Saturday. 
  7. Margaret Parker's Strathspey (S) - This dance, devised by John Drewry, is also on the Region Ball programme. It seemed a bit tricky to dance, but it looks rather nice when you see the video. https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/4155/ 
  8. Trip to Tmber Ridge (R) - To finish, Maureen gave us the choice between Catch the Wind or Trip to Timber Ridge. The latter was obviously the favourite. Such a great dance! https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/14903/ This is on the Region Ball programme. Great music, which Hilary says is devilishly hard to play! She will be playing this at the Region Ball. 

Corners
  • When the dancing couple is in second place, their first corner is diagonally across on their right, and their second corner is diagonally across on their left.
  • Imagine holding your arms spread out, your right hand points at your 1st corner, and your left hand points at your 2nd corner. 
  • Your corners will (almost) always be the opposite gender. 
  • In some dances, the 1s will be facing their corners, from 2nd place on the sidelines.
  • But in many dances, the 1s will be in the middle of the set, back-to-back with each other, and facing the corners (on the diagonal). 

Pas de Basque
I described this step in detail in my email of 28 August. For now, just this reminder of how it goes: https://youtu.be/H0_lkdhMikI 
  • Starting on the right foot:  R, L, R, pause; L, R, L, pause. 
  • Place R foot down; place L foot in front of the R foot (in 3rd position, T shape) and raise the R foot off the floor; shift your weight back onto the R foot again, and raise the L foot (pause); repeat, starting with the L foot.

    Debbie Roxburgh, Member of the New Zealand Order of Merit (MNZM)
    Today, the Governor General, Dame Cindy Kiro, presented Debbie Roxburgh with her MNZM for services to Scottish Country Dancing. 


    Debbie is the President of the NZ Branch of the RSCDS, and we are rightly very proud of all she has done for SCD in NZ.

    Below is the text that accompanied this photo on Facebook today:  

    Mrs Debbie Roxburgh, of Whanganui, MNZM for services to Scottish country dancing.
    Mrs Debbie Roxburgh teaches at the Glendarroch Scottish Country Dance Club in Whanganui. She teaches children, adults and experienced dancers with the New Zealand branch of the Royal Scottish Country Dance Society (RSCDS). She is an examiner for the RSCDS and regularly conducts examinations for new teachers in New Zealand and Australia. She has been Youth Co-ordinator, Rules Committee Convenor, and member of the New Zealand Branch Management Committee, and is President of the New Zealand RSCDS branch. Mrs Roxburgh helped establish a syllabus for medal test examinations for junior dancers, and Junior Associate Membership. She has helped organise week-long residential schools in New Zealand, and has taught at summer schools in Scotland.
