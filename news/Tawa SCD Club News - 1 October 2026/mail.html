Tawa SCD Club News - 1 October 2026

Hello everyone

Welcome back to Catherine - we hope you had a great holiday. Catherine expressed her thanks to Maureen for so capably holding the fort during her absence. We, the members, appreciated having Maureen to teach us too. 

We had a lovely night's dancing, with just on three sets. Catherine continues to introduce new formations for our newer dancers (several of whom, sadly, were missing). Last night's emphasis was on corners, Ladies' Chain, and "teapots". She skipped the pas de Basque practise "because tonight's a Tartan Night" ... Everyone danced the first five dances before supper. Thank you to Caroline and Anne for organising the supper for our Tartan night. The newer dancers went home after supper, and the rest of us danced a further two dances. 

Last night's dances


  1. The Dancing Bees (R) - This was our walking warm-up dance. It includes reels of 3 across the set. Devised by Roy Goldring for a couple whose surname was Bee, so nothing to do with buzzy insects ...
  2. Dame Margaret's Jig (J) - Devised by Wellington deviser Romaine Butterfield for Dame Margaret Sparrow, who used to dance in Kelburn and in Ngaio. This simple dance has the 1s dance "Adv and Ret" (advance and retire) with their corners. 
  3. The Paisley Weavers (S) - Before dancing this, we learned/practised the Ladies' Chain. The dance starts with two chords: the first is for the usual bow/curtsey, the second is for the 2nd couple to cross to their opposite sides. 
  4. The March Hare (R) - Devised by Iain Boyd to teach corners. In bars 17-24, the 1s set twice to their 1st corners, then swivel to set twice to their 2nd corners. 
  5. The Music Makars (R) - (Makars is not a spelling mistake, it's the name of a band). This dance introduced a new formation for our newer dancers: 3 Hands across at each end - also known as "teapots". It also includes a balance in line formation.  
  6. Asilomar Romantic (S) - Asilomar is the venue of an annual SCD workshop run by the San Francisco Branch. Do read the Extra Info for the story of unrequited love, told by the dance ...
  7. Scott Meikle (R) - With just a few minutes left, Catherine let us choose a dance we all knew - Anne was the first to pipe up with "Scott Meikle", which met with everyone's approval.

Glendarroch Club Annual Dance
Last Saturday Hilary and I drove to Whanganui to attend Glendarroch's Annual Dance. Other Tawa members were Anne and Debbie G, who enjoyed being a spectator; and Duncan and Mary, who of course was up on stage with her Saltire Band. The dance was well attended with seven sets, including at least one set's worth of children from the Glendarroch children's class. Because of the children, all the dances were walked, which was great as there were several unfamiliar dances on the programme. Glendarroch's teacher, and NZ Branch President, Debbie Roxburgh was the MC, with Christopher Burrows briefing a few dances, so Debbie could enjoy dancing too. The lively band, consisting of Mary and Glenys Saunders on fiddles and Lynne Scott on keyboard, kept us all energised, and an impressive and plentiful afternoon tea was very welcome after ten dances, to reinvigorate us for a further seven dances. The club ran a raffle for which the prizes were plants and seedlings - a bit different from the usual raffle fare. It was a long drive to Whanganui, and even longer, it seemed, going home, but well worth it. We had a great time.
