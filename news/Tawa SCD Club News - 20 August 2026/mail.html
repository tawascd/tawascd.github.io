Tawa SCD Club News - 20 August 2026

Hello everyone

Well some of us had a wonderful time at Ngaio Club's Dinner and Dance. It was disappointing that Tawa was not terribly well represented. Five of us attended the dinner and three more came to dance. We have to give full marks to Sean and especially Michelle L for coming along to dance. Despite having attended only four of our introductory classes, Michelle was very brave to join in with dances that must have seemed very confusing to her, but she did really well. That is one of the things I like best about SC dancers - everyone is friendly and helpful, and nobody gets cross when mistakes are made. We were all beginners once, and it takes time to get the hang of it all. 

The evening started with a delicious catered dinner and dessert. Quite a few people had dressed to the theme of the beach or the seaside - sunhats, shorts, sundresses, and even a quintessential Englishman with his trousers rolled up ready for a paddle, and wearing a knotted, polka-dotted hankie on his head (Robert Vale). John took photos, but he had a fall last night and is still feeling a bit shaky, so hasn't processed them yet. If any of them are useable, I will attach them next week. 

Melva had a great programme of dances, inspired by Peter Elmes' CD "Live at Ōpōtiki", which had a lot of beach- and sea-related titles on it. Jason and Hilary played these tunes magnificently, of course. The Sailor, Beach Dancer, Catch the Wind and Pelorus Jack were familiar to most. Culla Bay and The Water of Leith were a bit trickier, and The Fisherman's Reel was a fun five-couple dance in a square set with a "piggy-in-the-middle" couple that resulted in a bit of chaos and a lot of laughter at times. There was a quiz with sea- and geography-related questions, which was won by Hilary, to great applause.
