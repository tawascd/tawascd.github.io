Hello everyone

Wasn't it nice to see the sun the last few days? Not quite so cold tonight, but according to the forecast we could be in for another few days of southerly blasts. But spring can't be too far away - some of the spring bulbs in my garden have come up already. 

Tonight was the fourth (of five) introductory class, and though three the original beginners sent apologies (and assurances they will be back next week), we were pleased to see Angelina back, and she did very well. However, despite Duncan's and my best efforts, we couldn't persuade her daughter and her partner to join in with the dancing. 

In the first hour, with the beginners, we had step practise of skip change, strathspey setting and travelling steps. And we reviewed the Allemande, and learned how to step up, or down, correctly. We did the first four dances on the list with the beginners, the last three dances were for the rest of us. 

Tonight's dances 
  1. All Right (J) - This was the walking warm-up dance. 
  2. Allemande to Go (R) - This dance was devised by Gaye Collin specially to teach the Allemande.https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/16844/ 
  3. Sandy O'er the Lea (S) -  https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/5889/ We danced a slightly modified version. We did this last week. 
  4. Lady Catherine Bruce's Reel (J) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/3594/ Though it's called a reel, it is actually a jig. Another dance with an Allemande. 
  5. Farewell to Balfour Road (J) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/18272/  A 5C dance in a 5 C set, in which the top 2 couples dance hands round, while the three at the bottom dance reels (with other bits in between). It is a challenge and fun. It is on the programme for the Upper Hutt dance. 
  6. The Flower of Glasgow (S) -  https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/12941/   This dance includes a "circulating Allemande", in which the end couples dance towards the top, while the middle couple dances towards the bottom of the set. 
  7. The Royal Deeside Railway (R) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/9110/ This is on the programme of the Waikanae dance. Catherine explained that the name refers to the railway of Royal Deeside (a place favoured by the royal family, near Balmoral). The "royal" bit of the name refers to the place, not the railway.

Levin's 70th Anniversary Dance
Eight of our members enjoyed a very successful dance last Saturday (three of them are members, but we haven't seen them at club this year!). The dance was held in a beautiful, very large hall in the Speldhurst Lifestyle Village, near Levin. The hall easily accommodated the nine sets of dancers. Mary McDonald's band, The Saltire Scottish Dance Band, including Mary and Glenice on fiddles, Sharlene on keyboard, Sam on bass, and Duncan on drums, made a very full sound, easily filling the large space. The MCs were Melva Waite and Isobel Harris-Clark. A plentiful and delicious afternoon tea at 'half-time' was supplemented by birthday cake. 

You can see Joy Tracey's photos and a video of the Grand March on her FaceBook page https://www.facebook.com/search/top?q=jet%20scottish%20country%20dance%20photos  . And Pat Reesby, who was unable to dance on the night, recorded videos of several of the dances. You can find them on her YouTube page https://www.youtube.com/c/patreesby
