In last night's new dancers' section
The focus for the new dancers was on 
  • Right shoulder reels of 3
  • "Setting" or "Pas de Basque" step
  • Review dancing in a circle in Strathspey time

Right shoulder reels of 3
New Year Jig (dance no. 2) included a RSh reel. The dance starts with 1s dancing a RShoulder Fig of 8, which then flowed into a RSH reel of three. The 1s dance the exact same path in the reel as they did in the Fig 8; 1s and 2s pass by the Right shoulder, while the 3s move to their right to start the reel. 
The RSH reels of 3 also feature in Nos. 4 and 5. 

Pas de Basque 
This is the fifth and last step in Scottish country dancing. The others are: Slip step, skip change of step, strathspey travelling step, and strathspey setting step. 
We practised Pas de Basque in Reel tempo, 4/4, i.e. four even beats.  
  • Catherine demonstrated the step, which has three beats and a pause on the fourth beat. 
  • Then we tried the step, starting on the right foot:  R, L, R, pause; L, R, L, pause. 
  • Place R foot down; place L foot in front of the R foot (in 3rd position, T shape) and raise the R foot off the floor; shift your weight back onto the R foot again, and raise the L foot (pause); repeat, starting with the L foot. 
  • We started stepping it out slowly, and increasing in tempo, and finally doing it to the music.  
  • I think it helps if you don't put your full weight on the foot that goes in front. Just try it as if you had a stone in your shoe, and you want to lift your foot off quickly. 
  • In the video you'll see that the dancers kick the foot forward on the last beat (this is called the "jete"), but for now it is enough to just pause on that beat, until you've got the hang of the rhythm. https://youtu.be/H0_lkdhMikI 
Pas de Basque, also called 'setting', is used as a 'greeting', so usually when you set to someone, the other person will set back to you. "Setting" once, consists of two bars - once starting on the R foot and once on the L foot.
Tonight, Dances 3,4, and 5 included setting.

Circle in Strathspey time
Review: The steps are travelling steps, which usually move you forward. In a circle, however, you have to move sideways. In a circle, one usually circles to the left first, but one always starts with the right foot. So the right foot has to cross to the left to begin the circle. The trick here is that though your feet are going sideways, your body should still be facing into the circle. https://youtu.be/L_3BgigdQO4

    Johnsonville and Capital City Combined Annual Dance
    More than a dozen Tawa members attended this very successful dance, which also celebrated Jville's 60 years since its affilliation with the (R)SCDS and 30 years of Rod as its tutor. The Crofton Downs School hall easily fitted the nine sets in attendance. The stage was beautifully decorated with floral displays, arranged by Jville member Aline Homes and her helpers. The band, consisting of Lynne Scott, Heather Elder, Rebecca Bourne and Katie Scott provided the lively music. The 18 dances were briefed in 3-dance lots, alternating between Johnsonville's tutor Rod Downey, and Capital City's tutor Jeanette Watson. A set of Johnsonville dancers demonstrated "The St Columba's Church Strathspey", before everyone else dancing it. It is a very nice dance that Rod had devised especially for this occasion, to celebrate the club's beginnings, 60 years ago, in Jville's St Columba's Church hall (which now  no longer exists). In the 60 years, a large number of local tutors have taught at Johnsonville at various times, 11 of whom were able to attend, and were all presented with a small white cyclamen plant, among them Catherine, Maureen and Melva. Tawa's former tutor Maureen Robson also taught at Johnsonville on occasion, but was unable to attend, following cataract surgery. For a full report with lots more photos, go to the link on Johnsonville's website (report and photos by Loralee Hyde) https://www.johnsonvillescd.org.nz/2026/08/28/johnsonville-capital-city-annual-dance-2026/
