Last night's dances
  1. Come What May (J) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/17900/ This was our walking warm-up dance. Devised by Romaine Butterfield - formerly a teacher of the Island Bay (now Capital City) Club, and later of the Waikanae Club.  
  2. Will Starr (J) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/14811/ Devised by Johnsonville teacher Rod Downey, to teach the ladies' chain.
  3. The Flowers of Edinburgh (R) -  https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/2250/ This is an old dance, dating back to the 18th century, and published in the very first of the RSCDS books in 1924. It is universally popular, as you can tell from the large number of videos on the link. Before dancing this, we learned/practised the Poussette (see details below). 
  4. Balmoral Strathspey (S) -  https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/373/ Before dancing this we learned/practised a reel of four (pass R Sh at the ends, L Sh in the middle)  
  5. Summer on the Beach (R) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/17895/
  6. Catch the Wind (R) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/1087/ Devised by Romaine Butterfield for Iain Boyd and his wife Noeline O'Connor, whose courtship the dance describes.
  7. The Scallywag (J) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/9800/ 
  8. The Hazel Tree (J) - https://my.strathspey.org/dd/dance/2822/ Devised by John Drewry. 

Poussette
The name comes from the French verb "pousser", meaning "to push". ("poussette" is French for a child's pushchair). https://youtu.be/TEXJh0Sj-K0 
Salient points:
  • Done with two couples. 
  • Couples join both hands, held slightly wider than shoulder width. Firm arms - no "spaghetti arms". 
  • It is a progression, i.e. when done, the two couples are in reversed places
  • It is danced in a square. Each couple dances half of the square: the 1s dance towards the men's side to begin, the 2s dance towards the ladies' side.
  • The "jingle" that goes with this formation is: "Away from the centre, quarter turn; up or down, quarter turn; into the centre, half turn round; dance back, dance back". Each of those elements takes one bar, i.e. one PdB step.  
  • The person who finds themselves in the corner just pivots on the spot, while their partner dances the quarter turn. 
  • You are either moving or turning, but never both at the same time.

Region Advanced Class - John Drewry Dances
Held last Saturday 17 October. Damon Collin taught a very interesting class which was quite challenging - both physically and mentally. John Drewry was a prolific deviser with more than 760 dances to his name. It was a mammoth job for Damon, who said he'd spent the last six months poring over them to select the seven dances for this class. There were just two sets, with Damon and Gaye both having to dance to complete the sets. From Tawa, Hilary, Joy and I attended. Elaine Lethbridge provided afternoon tea, assisted by Anne Hickling who had come along to observe. We stopped for tea half-way through learning one of the dances - we needed that break!
